# The Importance of Whitespace in Design

Whitespace is the space around and between elements in a design, such as text, images, and buttons. It helps create visual balance, makes content easier to understand, and guides the user's attention.

Despite its name, **whitespace does not have to be white**. It refers to empty or unoccupied space in a layout, regardless of the background color.

## 1. Why Whitespace Matters

A page can contain all the right information and still be difficult to use if its elements are crowded together. Whitespace gives those elements room to breathe and helps people understand how the page is organized.

Whitespace can help to:

- **Create balance** between elements on a page.
- **Improve readability** by separating text and other content.
- **Establish visual hierarchy** by making some elements stand out more than others.
- **Guide attention** toward important information or actions.
- **Group related content** and distinguish it from unrelated content.

The goal is not to add as much empty space as possible. It is to use space intentionally so the page feels clear and well organized.

## 2. Using Whitespace to Emphasize Calls to Action

A **call to action (CTA)** is an element that encourages the user to take a specific action, such as signing up for a newsletter or making a purchase. A CTA might be a button labeled “Get started” or “Buy now.”

Whitespace can make a CTA more noticeable by separating it from surrounding content.

For example, on the freeCodeCamp homepage, the “Get started (it's free)” button is positioned with space around it. This separation helps the button stand out instead of competing with nearby elements.

![Call-to-action button on the freeCodeCamp homepage with yellow background and black text reading: Get started (it's free). The button is centered on its own line with ample white space above and below.](https://cdn.freecodecamp.org/curriculum/lecture-transcripts/what-is-the-importance-of-whitespace-in-design-1.png)

### Practical takeaway

When designing a CTA:

- Leave enough space around it so it is easy to notice.
- Keep it visually distinct from nearby text, images, and other controls.
- Avoid crowding it with competing elements.

Whitespace can draw attention to a CTA, but it does not guarantee that users will click it. The button's wording, placement, and relevance also matter.

## 3. Types of Whitespace

Whitespace can be described in four ways: **macro**, **micro**, **active**, and **passive** whitespace. These terms describe different aspects of how space appears or is used in a layout.

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Macro whitespace** | Space between larger elements or sections. | The gap between a heading, an image, and a paragraph, or between two content sections. |
| **Micro whitespace** | Small spaces within or around text and other compact elements. | Spacing between characters or words, and the space between lines of text. |
| **Active whitespace** | Space deliberately created to guide attention or give important content emphasis. | Leaving extra room around a primary CTA or between sections to guide the reader through a page. |
| **Passive whitespace** | Space that remains around elements as part of the layout, without being deliberately used to emphasize a particular item. | The small gaps that naturally remain between words or around content. |

Macro and micro whitespace describe the **scale** of the space. Active and passive whitespace describe its **role or intention**. A space can therefore be both macro and active, for example.

### Macro whitespace

Macro whitespace operates at the level of larger layout elements. It separates sections, text blocks, images, and buttons.

**Why it matters:** Without enough macro whitespace, a page can feel crowded and users may struggle to tell where one section ends and another begins.

### Micro whitespace

Micro whitespace concerns smaller spaces, especially those within text. The spacing between characters, words, and lines can affect how comfortable text is to read.

The freeCodeCamp Frequently Asked Questions section below illustrates how spacing helps separate and present text clearly.

![The Frequently Asked Questions section on the freeCodeCamp homepage, with text spaced sufficiently between each letter.](https://cdn.freecodecamp.org/curriculum/lecture-transcripts/what-is-the-importance-of-whitespace-in-design-2.png)

**Why it matters:** Appropriate micro whitespace improves readability and legibility, making text easier to scan and understand. Too little or poorly chosen spacing can make text feel cramped; too much can make it harder to see which words belong together.

### Active whitespace

Active whitespace is intentionally used as part of the design. It helps direct the user's eye, highlight important content, or create a clearer path through the page.

**Example:** Giving a primary CTA extra room can make it stand out from secondary content.

### Passive whitespace

Passive whitespace is space that remains in the layout without being deliberately assigned a specific emphasis or guiding role.

Passive does not mean useless. Even space that is not intended to highlight something can contribute to a layout that feels less crowded and easier to read.

## 4. The Law of Proximity

The **law of proximity** is a design principle stating that elements placed close together tend to be perceived as related, while elements placed farther apart tend to be perceived as separate.

Whitespace helps communicate these relationships visually, often without needing additional labels or explanatory text.

### Example

Imagine a page with three questions and their answers:

- If each question is close to its answer, readers are more likely to understand that they belong together.
- If there is a larger gap between one question-and-answer pair and the next, readers can more easily recognize the separate groups.

### How to apply the law of proximity

- Keep related items close together.
- Add more space between unrelated groups or sections.
- Use consistent spacing for elements that serve the same purpose.
- Avoid placing related text so far apart that the relationship becomes unclear.

**Important:** Whitespace does more than make a page look attractive. It helps communicate the structure of the information.

## 5. Practical Checklist

When reviewing a web page or interface, ask:

- [ ] Is there enough space between major sections?
- [ ] Are related elements grouped together?
- [ ] Are unrelated groups separated clearly?
- [ ] Does the primary CTA have enough space to stand out?
- [ ] Is text comfortably spaced and easy to read?
- [ ] Is the spacing consistent enough to make the layout feel organized?
- [ ] Does the page feel clear rather than crowded or unnecessarily spread out?

## Summary

| Concept | Remember |
|---|---|
| **Whitespace** | Empty space around and between elements; it does not have to be white. |
| **Macro whitespace** | Space between larger elements and sections. |
| **Micro whitespace** | Small-scale spacing, especially within text. |
| **Active whitespace** | Space deliberately used to guide attention or create emphasis. |
| **Passive whitespace** | Space that remains as part of the layout without a specific emphasis goal. |
| **CTA** | A call to action that encourages the user to do something. Whitespace can help it stand out. |
| **Law of proximity** | Elements close together tend to be perceived as related; elements farther apart tend to be perceived as separate. |

## Key Takeaways

- Whitespace is an important part of a design, not simply unused room.
- Use space to create balance, improve readability, and guide attention.
- Give important actions, such as CTAs, enough room to be noticeable.
- Use macro whitespace to organize larger sections and micro whitespace to support comfortable reading.
- Use the law of proximity to show which elements belong together.
- Aim for intentional spacing, not simply more spacing.
